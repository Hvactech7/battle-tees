Stableford

Points for your score vs par — highest wins

The game in 20 seconds

Everyone plays their own ball and scores points against par on every hole: par 2, birdie 3, eagle 4, albatross 5, bogey 1, double bogey or worse 0. Most points wins.

A blow-up hole costs no more than a double, so you can go for it without wrecking the round.

Who plays

  • One to four players. Battle Tees sets the count from the golfers in your group.
  • No teams and no order. Everyone races everyone.
  • On your own it's you against the course — there's nobody to settle with.

Scoring a hole

  • Compare the score to the hole's par and take the points from the table.
  • Three under par or better — a hole-in-one on a par 4 — scores the albatross points.
  • Back at the cart, enter the scores and confirm. The points add up hole by hole.
Hole 1 · par 4 · grossNate 4 (par) → 2. Greg 3 (birdie) → 3.
Mike 5 (bogey) → 1. Tyler 7 (triple) → 0.

Blow-ups stop at zero

Once you're past double bogey the hole can't cost you anything more. A triple, a quad and a 10 all score 0.

Hole 2 · par 5 · grossNate 3 (eagle) → 4. Greg 5 → 2. Mike 6 → 1.
Tyler 8 → 0 — exactly what a 7 would have scored.
Hole 3 · par 3 · grossNate 3 → 2. Greg 4 → 1. Mike 4 → 1. Tyler 5 → 0.
After three holes: Nate 8, Greg 6, Mike 3, Tyler 0.

Every value in the table is yours to set in setup, from −5 to 10. Put a minus on bogey and double, and blow-ups hurt again — totals can even finish below zero.

Handicaps

Net Stableford uses each player's full course handicap, like every net game. A 25 gets 25 strokes: one on every hole and a second on the 7 hardest, by the card's stroke index. A plus handicap gives strokes back on the easiest holes.

Hole 1 · par 4 · net (stroke index 7)Course handicaps 4, 10, 18, 25: Greg and Mike get a stroke here, Tyler two, Nate none.
Nate 4 → net par 2. Greg 3 → net eagle 4.
Mike 5 → net par 2. Tyler 7 → net bogey 1.

Net, the same three holes come out Nate 9, Greg 8, Mike 6, Tyler 3.

Battle Tees works out each course handicap from the player's index and the tees they played, at the group's handicap allowance (100% unless you change it). The Standings show gross and net points side by side.

💰The money

Stableford is paid player against player. Every two players settle the difference between their point totals, at the price per point.

Net, after holes 1–3, at 50¢ a pointNate 9, Greg 8, Mike 6, Tyler 3.
Nate is ahead of all three by 1 + 3 + 6 points → +$5.00.
Greg wins 2 from Mike and 5 from Tyler, loses 1 to Nate → +$3.00.
Mike −$1.00. Tyler −$7.00.

Set the price per point under Settle up on the Standings screen; it lists who pays whom. Battle Tees never touches the money.

Options in Battle Tees

  • Players in the round — 1 to 4, on its card when it's your main game; as a side game it follows the group.
  • Birdie 3 · Par 2 · Bogey 1 · Double bogey or worse 0 · Eagle 4 · Albatross 5 — each from −5 to 10.

Set them in setup before the round. Every phone in the group plays with the same settings.

⚡How to win it

  • Go for the par 5s. A birdie is a point more than par; a double costs two, and nothing worse costs more.
  • Once double is gone, so is the hole. Every bigger number scores the same 0 — don't burn energy saving a triple.
  • Use your strokes. On a stroke hole a gross bogey is a net par: 2 points, not 1.
  • Know the table. If your group runs negatives, a blow-up costs again and the safe play comes back.

Variations you'll hear about

Modified Stableford. The PGA Tour's version pays albatross 8, eagle 5, birdie 2, par 0, bogey −1, double or worse −3. Battle Tees starts on the classic table, but all six fit on the steppers — on holes 1–3 above, that table gives Nate 5, Greg 1, Mike −3, Tyler −9.

95% handicaps. Individual Stableford competitions under the World Handicap System use 95% of course handicap. Battle Tees uses 100% unless you lower the handicap allowance — 95% is one tap down.

🔥Why it rewards the brave

Stableford is the antidote to defensive golf. It was invented precisely because stroke play punishes ambition, and it turns a round into a series of independent gambles rather than one long exercise in damage control.

The scoring is also just satisfying. Points accumulate, the number only goes up, and a great hole feels great in a way that "one under" never quite captures.

It's the format that produces the most "go for it" moments per round — and in a money game, those are the shots everyone remembers.
