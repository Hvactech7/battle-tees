Sixes

Partners rotate every six holes — everyone teams with everyone

The game in 20 seconds

Four players, two teams of two — and the teams change every six holes, so by the 18th everybody has partnered everybody. You'll also hear it called Round Robin or Hollywood.

On each hole the better ball of each team wins it, and both winners get 1 point. The points are yours, not the team's: at the end you settle with each of the other three.

Partners and the rotation

  • Pick the starting partners — any of the three ways to split four players. Once all four have joined, the captain gets a Confirm the partners sheet before the first hole is scored.
  • Swap every six. The other two pairings play the next two sixes. Start with Nate & Greg v Mike & Tyler, and holes 7–12 are Nate & Mike v Greg & Tyler, holes 13–18 Nate & Tyler v Greg & Mike.
  • Nine holes? It swaps every 3 — three pairings, three holes each.
  • Choose each six yourself (option) — the Sixes card in Standings lists every six with its three pairings. Use one twice and the app warns you two players will never partner. A six that has started is set; the ones still to come can change.

Scoring a hole

  • Better ball wins. Each team's lower score is its ball for the hole.
  • 1 point to each winner. Both partners book it.
  • Better balls tie? Add both balls — the lower team total wins the hole.
  • Still tied — push. Nobody scores, and nothing carries.

Nate & Greg win 1 point each.

Ties

A tie on the better ball goes to both balls. Only when the totals tie as well is the hole a push.

Hole 2 · par 4Nate 4, Greg 5. Mike 4, Tyler 6 — the better balls tie at 4.
Both balls: 9 against 10. Nate & Greg win 1 point each.
Hole 3 · par 5Nate 5, Greg 6. Mike 5, Tyler 6.
5 against 5, then 11 against 11 — a push. Nobody scores.

Handicaps

Play it net and every player gets their full course handicap — one stroke a hole on the hardest holes first, by the card's stroke index. The best player gets his strokes too. Dots on the score screen mark the holes where you get one.

Your strokes go with you to every partner, and net scores decide the tiebreak too — a stroke on the second ball can win a hole.

A net hole · par 4Greg gets a stroke. Nate 4, Greg 5 (net 4). Mike 4, Tyler 5.
Gross: 4 v 4, then 9 v 9 — push. Net: 4 v 4, then 8 v 9 — Nate & Greg win.

Battle Tees works out each player's course handicap from their index and the tees they played, at the group's handicap allowance (100% unless you change it in setup). Gross and net are kept side by side all round.

💰The money

Sixes settles player against player. Everyone ends with their own points, and every pair of players settles the difference — so each hole you win collects the price from both players you beat. At $1 a point, a hole won is $2 to each winner.

After 18, at $1 a pointNate 8 points, Greg 7, Mike 6, Tyler 5.
Tyler pays Nate $3, Greg $2 and Mike $1. Mike pays Nate $2 and Greg $1. Greg pays Nate $1.
Nate +$6, Greg +$2, Mike −$2, Tyler −$6.

Battle Tees shows who owes whom under Settle up on the Standings screen; it never touches the money.

Options in Battle Tees

  • Starting partners — one of the three pairings. Default: the first two players on the list against the last two.
  • Partners for each six — on the Sixes card in Standings. Default: the rotation from the starting partners.
  • 🔨 Add The Hammer? — No thanks unless you pick Yes — hammer's in. Either team can then double a hole; a doubled hole pays each winner 2 points. See the Hammer card.

Partners and the Hammer are settled before the first hole; the six-by-six picker lives in Standings. Every phone in the group plays with the same settings.

⚡How to win it

  • Finish every hole. When the better balls tie, the second ball decides it — your bogey behind your partner's par can still win the point.
  • You're playing all three. You settle with everyone, so the partner you're helping on 8 is a player you're trying to beat on 14.
  • A bad draw lasts six holes. Your points travel with you — keep your own card clean and the next six is a fresh start.

Variations you'll hear about

Six-hole matches. Some groups bet each six as its own match — the team that wins more holes in the six wins the bet. Battle Tees pays every hole instead.

🔥Why it settles every argument

Every group has the same recurring fight: the pairings weren't fair. Sixes ends it permanently. Everyone partners everyone for exactly six holes, so the format is mathematically above suspicion.

It also produces three distinct mini-rounds inside one round. The dynamic genuinely changes when partners swap — new strategy, new banter, new alliances — and the guy who was your enemy on the front nine is depending on you by hole 13.

Best of all, it keeps the last six holes meaningful for everybody. Nobody is locked into a losing partnership as the round dies out.
