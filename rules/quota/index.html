Quota

Race your own number — the fairest game for mixed handicaps

The game in 20 seconds

Everyone gets a target — their quota: 36 minus their course handicap. Then each hole scores points off your gross score: par 2, birdie 3, eagle 4, albatross 6, bogey 1, double bogey or worse 0. Whoever finishes furthest over their quota wins.

The handicap lives in the target, so a 25 and a 4 each race their own number.

Your quota

  • 18 holes: 36 minus your course handicap. A 4 plays to 32, a 25 to 11.
  • 9 holes: 18 minus half your course handicap, rounded — a 25 plays to 6.
  • A plus handicap raises it: a +2 plays to 38.
  • One to four players, no teams. The Quota card in setup shows everyone's number before you tee off.

Scoring a hole

  • Points come from your gross score against par — no strokes on holes.
  • Bogey scores 1; double bogey or worse scores 0.
  • Three under par or better — a hole-in-one on a par 4 — scores the albatross 6.
  • Back at the cart, enter the scores and confirm. The points add up hole by hole.

Mike 5 (bogey) → 1. Tyler 6 → 0.

Plus or minus

Your score is points minus quota. The Standings show Pts, Quota and +/− for everyone, furthest over on top.

Mid-round the +/− climbs up from minus your quota — after that first hole Nate (quota 32) is −29 and Tyler (quota 11) is −11. Read it as how far you still have to go.

After 18 holesNate shoots 71: 37 points on a 32 quota → +5.
Greg 76: 32 on 26 → +6.
Mike 81: 27 on 18 → +9.
Tyler 95: 13 on 11 → +2.
Mike wins with the third-best score of the day — he beat his number by the most.

Handicaps

Quota builds the handicap into the target, so there are no strokes on holes. Points always come from your gross score, so the score screen shows no dots for Quota.

The target uses your full course handicap: the handicap allowance in setup doesn't change it. Battle Tees works out each course handicap from the player's index and the tees they played.

💰The money

Quota pays on the +/−, not the raw points. Every two players settle the difference between their +/−, at the price per point.

That round, at $1 a pointMike +9, Greg +6, Nate +5, Tyler +2.
Mike takes 3 from Greg, 4 from Nate and 7 from Tyler → +$14.
Greg +$2. Nate −$2. Tyler −$14.
Paid in the fewest moves: Tyler → Mike $14, Nate → Greg $2.
On raw points Tyler would owe Nate $24. On the +/− it's $3.

Set the price per point under Settle up on the Standings screen; it lists who pays whom. Battle Tees never touches the money.

Options in Battle Tees

  • Players in the round — 1 to 4, on the Quota card when Quota is your main game.
  • Par 2 · Birdie 3 · Eagle 4 · Albatross 6 · Bogey 1 — each from −5 to 10. Double bogey or worse is always 0.

Changing the points doesn't change anyone's quota. Set them in setup before the round. Every phone in the group plays with the same settings.

⚡How to win it

  • Bogey golf makes quota. At a point a bogey, an 18 who bogeys every hole finishes dead even. Every par on top of that is a point up.
  • Save the bogey. A bogey is 1 and a double is 0 — out of position, take your medicine and keep the point.
  • A birdie is a par and a half. 3 points against 2 — go after the short par 4s and the reachable par 5s.
  • Read the +/− late. With three to play, the gap to the leader tells you whether you need a birdie or just pars.

Variations you'll hear about

Bigger birdies. Many groups play bogey 1, par 2, birdie 4, eagle 8. Battle Tees starts at birdie 3 and eagle 4 — set Birdie 4 and Eagle 8 to play it that way. The quota stays 36 minus handicap.

The Chicago. That same ladder with quotas of 39 minus handicap. Battle Tees always uses 36.

🔥Why it's the fairest game here

Quota is the rare format where everybody plays their own race. Your target is your target; the scratch player's is theirs. Nobody is taking strokes off anybody, nobody is arguing about the dots, and a 25-handicap can beat a 4 without either of them feeling like the format did it.

It also works solo, which almost nothing else here does. You against your own number on a quiet Tuesday evening is a genuinely good round of golf.

And there's a specific satisfaction to being told, on the first tee, exactly what you're expected to shoot — and then beating it.
