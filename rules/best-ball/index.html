Best Ball

2 v 2 match play — your team’s best score counts

The game in 20 seconds

Four players, two teams of two. Everyone plays their own ball, and on each hole the better score on each team is the team's score. Lower score wins the hole. It's match play — win a hole and you're 1 UP — and the money is paid on holes won.

Picking teams

  • Pick the partners in setup — any of the three ways to split four players — and name the teams if you like. Once all four are in, the captain's phone asks to confirm the partners.
  • 🎲 Mystery partners (option) — when Best Ball is a side game, nobody knows the teams until the round is over. After the last hole anyone taps Reveal partners, and the whole card is scored with the teams it draws — the same on every phone.

Scoring a hole

  • Take each team's low score. One good ball is all a team needs.
  • Lower one wins the hole.
  • Same score? The hole is halved.
Hole 1 · par 4Nate 4, Greg 5 → 4. Mike 5, Tyler 6 → 5.
Nate & Greg win the hole: 1 UP.
Hole 2 · par 4Nate 5, Greg 6 → 5. Mike 5, Tyler 7 → 5.
Halved — still 1 UP.

The match

Standings keeps the match: 1 UP, all square, and dormie when a team leads by exactly the holes left. Lead by more than the holes left and it's won: 3&2 is 3 up with 2 to play.

Thru 16Nate & Greg are 3 UP after 15 with 3 to play — dormie. They halve 16.
Nate & Greg win 3&2.

The money stops there too. A match won 3&2 pays as won — 17 and 18 can still be played and scored, but they don't move the bet.

Handicaps

Play it net and the strokes come off the low man: the best player plays off zero, and everyone else gets the difference between their course handicap and the low man's — one stroke a hole on the hardest holes first, by the card's stroke index. Dots on the score screen mark the holes where you get one.

The number 1 handicap hole · par 4Course handicaps Nate 5, Greg 11, Mike 13, Tyler 8 — Greg, Mike and Tyler each get a stroke.
Nate 4, Greg 5 → net 4. Mike 5, Tyler 5 → net 4.
Gross, Nate & Greg win it. Net, it's halved.

Course handicaps come from each player's index and tees, at the group's handicap allowance (100% unless you change it in setup). Gross and net are kept side by side all round.

💰The money

Best Ball is a team bet paid by the hole: the difference in holes won, times the $ per hole, from the losing team — split between the partners.

That round, at $5 a holeNate & Greg win 3&2: 5 holes to 2 when the match ended on 16.
3 × $5 = $15: Mike and Tyler pay $7.50 each, Nate and Greg collect $7.50 each.
Mike & Tyler win 17 and 18 anyway — it's still $15.

Add the 🔨 Hammer and one hole can count 2, 4 or 8 times. Set the $ per hole under Settle up on the Standings screen; Battle Tees shows who owes whom and never touches the money.

Options in Battle Tees

  • 🔨 Add The Hammer? — No thanks unless you pick Yes — hammer's in.
  • 🎲 Mystery partners — draw at the end — Off. On the Best Ball card in Standings, for a side game with no Hammer.

⚡How to win it

  • Somebody has to be in play. Once your partner is safely on, you're free to attack the flag.
  • Decide on the tee. Settle who plays safe and who takes the risk before anyone swings.
  • Putt in the right order. Same line, further back? Let your partner go first and read the break for free.
  • Close it out. A 3-up lead with 3 to play only needs a halve — the next push ends it.

Variations you'll hear about

Betting the match. Many groups put one bet on the match itself — win 3&2, collect the bet. Battle Tees pays by the hole, every hole. Want front, back and overall bets? That's Nassau.

Low ball and low total. Some groups play two points a hole — low ball and the lower team total. Best Ball counts only the low ball; Umbrella adds the team total.

🔥Why it's the best format for friends

Four-ball is the format that lets a good player and a struggling player genuinely enjoy the same round. Your bad holes get absorbed by your partner, and your one great hole can win a match single-handedly — even if the rest of your card is a mess.

The clinch is the drama. Watching a match go dormie, then hearing "3&2" and knowing it's over with holes still to play, gives the round a finish line that stroke play never has.

And it's the natural home of the Hammer. Double-or-fold on top of match play is the most volatile combination in the app — a match that looked dead can come back on one thrown hammer.
