Scramble

One ball per team

The game in 20 seconds

Everyone on the team hits, you pick the best shot, and everyone plays the next one from there — all the way into the hole. One score per team per hole, and the lowest total wins.

Play it 2 v 2 for money, or as one team — two of you or all four — to keep a card.

Formats and teams

  • 2 v 2 — four players, two teams. Once all four have joined, the captain gets a Confirm the partners sheet before the first hole is scored.
  • Just us 2 — two players, one ball, one card. Nobody to play against.
  • All 4 — the whole group on one ball: a tournament card.
  • Scramble plays alone. No other game can ride on a scramble card — it holds team scores, not your own.

Scoring a hole

  • Everyone hits, the team picks the best ball, and everyone plays from that spot. Keep going until it's holed.
  • One score per team. Back at the cart, set it on the team's row — each new hole starts at par, tap − or + from there.
  • Add them up. The lowest team total over the round wins. There are no points and no holes won.

Handicaps — the team allowance

Scramble doesn't use plain strokes. Battle Tees builds a team handicap from the players' course handicaps, using the USGA's scramble allowances:

  • Two-player team: 35% of the lower course handicap plus 15% of the higher.
  • Four-player team: 25% of the lowest, 20% of the next, 15% of the next, 10% of the highest.
  • 2 v 2: the better team plays off zero, and the other team gets the difference — one stroke a hole on the hardest holes first, by the card's stroke index.
  • One team: nobody to play off, so the team takes its whole handicap. A plus team gives strokes back on the easiest holes.

All four on one ball: 25% of 4 + 20% of 8 + 15% of 12 + 10% of 20 = 6.4 → 6.

The group's handicap allowance (100% unless you change it in setup) scales the team handicap. Battle Tees works out each course handicap from the player's index and the tees they played, and keeps gross and net side by side all round.

🧮Texas scramble handicap — how to work it out

“Texas scramble” is what golfers in the UK and Ireland call a scramble. The handicap works the same either way, and it uses the standard scramble allowances:

2 players · 35% low + 15% high4 players · 25% · 20% · 15% · 10%

1. Get each player's course handicap for the tees you are playing — handicap index × slope ÷ 113, plus course rating minus par, rounded.

2. Sort them lowest to highest and apply the percentages in that order: the lowest handicap gets the biggest percentage.

3. Add them up and round to the nearest whole number. That is the team's handicap.

Worked example (four players): course handicaps 4, 8, 12 and 16 give 25% of 4 = 1.0, 20% of 8 = 1.6, 15% of 12 = 1.8, 10% of 16 = 1.6. Total 6.0 — team handicap 6. Some clubs round each player's share first, which here gives 1 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 7; check your competition's rules if a stroke matters.

Two-player example: 6 and 18 give 35% of 6 = 2.1 plus 15% of 18 = 2.7, so 4.8 — team handicap 5. In a 2 v 2 match the team handicaps are compared and the higher team gets the difference: 6 & 18 (5) against 14 & 20 (4.9 + 3.0 = 7.9, so 8) means the second team gets 3 strokes.

💰The money

2 v 2 is a team bet on strokes. At the end, the margin between the two team totals is the bet — counted once, and split between the two partners.

Scramble questions

What is a Texas scramble?
The same format as a scramble: everyone tees off, the team picks the best shot, and everyone plays from there. “Texas scramble” is the usual name in the UK and Ireland.

How many drives does each player need in a Texas scramble?
Many competitions require a minimum number of each player's drives over the round — often three or four each in an 18-hole four-player event. It is set by the organiser, so check the entry sheet.

Why isn't a scramble played off full handicaps?
Because the team always plays its best shot, a team scores far better than any one player would. Full handicaps would hand high-handicap teams an enormous edge, so the allowances shrink them.

What about a three-player scramble?
The standard allowances cover two- and four-player scrambles. Three-player events set their own, so check with the organiser.

⚡How to win it

  • Steady player first. Get one ball in play, then everyone else can swing hard.
  • Putt for the read. Whoever goes first shows the line — let your best putter go last.
  • Go for it. With two or four tries at every shot, take on the reachable par 5s and the short par 4s.
  • Pick position, not distance. The fairway 20 yards back often beats the rough up close.

Variations you'll hear about

Minimum drives. Many events make each player's drive count a set number of times over the round. Battle Tees doesn't track whose shot you used — keep that count yourselves.

🔥Why it's the most social round in golf

Scramble is the format where nobody has a bad day. Your worst shot disappears the second somebody else hits a good one, which means the pressure evaporates and the conversation starts. It's why every charity event and member-guest on earth runs one.

It's also the fastest way to bring a beginner into a money game. A new golfer who would be miserable playing their own ball can contribute one great drive on one hole and be a hero for the rest of the round.

And it's genuinely fun to watch a team gel — four people converging on one ball, arguing about the line, then all making the same putt in sequence.
