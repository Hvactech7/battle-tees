Vegas

2 v 2 — team digits duel, birdies flip the number

The game in 20 seconds

Four players, two teams of two. On every hole each team turns its two scores into one two-digit number, low score first: a 4 and a 5 make 45. The lower number wins the hole, and the winning team takes the difference in points — 45 against 57 is 12 points.

Make a birdie and the other team's number flips, high score first. That's where the big swings come from.

Picking teams

  • Pick the partners in setup — any of the three ways to split four players.
  • Swap partners every 6 holes (option) — holes 1–6, 7–12 and 13–18 each have a different pairing, so everybody partners everybody once. On a nine-hole round it swaps every 3.
  • 🎲 Mystery partners (option) — nobody knows the teams until the round is over. Everyone plays their own ball; after the last hole anyone taps Reveal partners, and the whole card is scored with the teams it draws — the same on every phone.

Scoring a hole

  • Low score first. A 4 and a 5 is 45. A 5 and a 7 is 57.
  • 10 or more counts as a 9, so a blow-up hole can never make a three-digit number: a 5 and a 12 is 59.
  • Lower number wins the difference. Both players on the winning team book the points.
  • Same number? Nobody scores on the hole.
Hole 1 · par 4Nate 4 + Greg 5 → 45. Mike 5 + Tyler 7 → 57.
Nate & Greg win 12 points each.

The birdie flip

If anyone on a team makes birdie or better, the other team's number flips — high score first. If both teams make a birdie, neither number flips.

Hole 2 · par 4Nate makes a 3 (birdie), Greg a 6 → 36.
Mike 5 + Tyler 8 would be 58 — Nate's birdie flips it to 85.
Nate & Greg win 49 points instead of 22.
Hole 3 · par 3Mike makes a 2 (birdie), Tyler a 4 → 24.
Nate 3 + Greg 3 is 33 — and flipped it's still 33.
Mike & Tyler win 9 points.

In a net game it's a net birdie that flips. The flip can be turned off in setup.

Handicaps

Play it net and the strokes come off the low man: the best player in the group plays off zero, and everyone else gets the difference between their course handicap and the low man's — one stroke a hole on the hardest holes first, by the card's stroke index. Dots on the score screen mark the holes where you get one.

A stroke is worth a lot in Vegas. One 5 that becomes a net 4 turns a 57 into a 47 — ten points on one hole.

Battle Tees works out each player's course handicap from their index and the tees they played, at the group's handicap allowance (100% unless you change it in setup). Gross and net are kept side by side all round.

💰The money

Vegas is a team bet. At the end, the difference between the two teams' points is the bet — counted once, and split between the two partners.

After those three holes, at 10¢ a pointNate & Greg 61 points, Mike & Tyler 9 — a 52-point margin.
52 × $0.10 = $5.20: Mike and Tyler pay $2.60 each, Nate and Greg collect $2.60 each.

One hole can move 50 points or more, so keep the price low — 5¢ or 10¢ a point goes a long way. Battle Tees shows who owes whom under Settle up on the Standings screen; it never touches the money.

Options in Battle Tees

  • Partners — pick one of the three pairings, and name the teams if you like.
  • Swap partners every 6 holes — Off unless you turn it on.
  • Birdie flips opponent's number — On.
  • Closest to pin +1 (par 3s) — Off. When it's on, the closest player's whole team gets the point.
  • 🎲 Mystery partners — teams drawn after the round.

Set them in setup before the round. Every phone in the group plays with the same settings.

⚡How to win it

  • Protect the low digit. The smaller score sets the tens column, so one good score matters more than two decent ones. When your partner is safely on, you're free to attack.
  • A dead hole is dead. Past 9 it's all a 9 — pick up and stop the bleeding.
  • Birdies are weapons. A flip is usually a bigger swing than the hole itself — go for the reachable par 5s.
  • With swaps on, bank points while you're paired with the hot hand.

Variations you'll hear about

No cap. Some groups let a 10 make a three-digit number — a 10 and a 4 is 104. Battle Tees caps every score at 9.

🔥Why the swings are absurd

Vegas produces the biggest numbers in golf. A single hole can move fifty points, and a birdie flip against a struggling team can move more than that. There is no such thing as a safe lead, which means nobody ever stops caring.

It also creates the funniest possible disasters. Your partner makes a 9, you make a 5, and suddenly the number on your team is 59 against their 34 — a twenty-five point hole that took four seconds to happen. Everyone will be talking about it in the parking lot.

The flip is the signature moment. Watching an opponent's face when a twenty-footer drops and their number inverts is the whole reason the game exists.
