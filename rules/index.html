Which game should your group play?

Foursome that wants drama every hole — Wolf. One public decision per hole, and the role rotates so nobody escapes it.

Threesome — 9's. Built for exactly three players; every hole pays somebody.

Two teams of two — Best Ball for a steady match, Vegas if you want swings that hurt, Sixes if you want everyone to partner everyone.

Wildly mixed handicaps — Quota or Stableford. Everyone races their own number and nobody argues about strokes.

You just want money on every hole — Skins. Ties carry, and one green late can pay for the round.

The classic wager — Nassau. Three bets in one round: front, back and overall, with presses when you're down.

Nobody trusts anybody's handicap — Bingo Bango Bongo. The order of play is the handicap, so it's fair without a single stroke.

Playing for money without the arguments

Every game here is scored in points — or in Skins' case, dollars per hole — and your group decides what a point is worth before the first tee. Battle Tees keeps gross and net side by side on every phone, so a 5 and a 20 can genuinely play each other, and the settle screen turns the whole round into one list of who owes whom.

The app never touches the money. It scores, it does the math, it shows you the number. You settle it in the parking lot, the way golfers always have.
