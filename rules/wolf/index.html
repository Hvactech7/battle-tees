Wolf

First off the tee hunts with a partner — or goes lone wolf

The game in 20 seconds

Four players, and every hole has a new Wolf. Pick a fixed playing order that rotates the starting player every hole. That starting player, is The Wolf for that hole. The Wolf hits first, watches the drives and picks a partner — or takes on all three alone. The best ball of each side wins the hole, and everyone on the winning side books points. Most points wins.

The Wolf rotates so everyone gets the same turns, and on 17 and 18 it goes to whoever is in last place.

The order of play

  • The order is the rotation. Once all four are in, the starter's phone asks you to Confirm the order of play — drag the golfers into tee order. First listed is the Wolf on the first hole, second on the next, and it starts over every four holes. ⇅ Fix tee order on the score screen works until the first hole is confirmed.
  • The Wolf always hits first; the others follow in the order.
  • Over the first 16 holes everyone is the Wolf four times.
  • Start on another tee or play nine and the rotation follows the holes you play. Pick Wolf up mid-round and it counts from that hole, with the first golfer in the order as the Wolf.

Playing a hole

  • Blind Lone Wolf — called before anyone hits, the Wolf included.
  • Otherwise the Wolf hits, then watches each drive and takes that player or passes. A pass is final.
  • Lone Wolf — no partner, one against three.
  • Back at the cart the Wolf taps the call: Blind lone wolf, Lone wolf, or the partner's name. Scores can't be confirmed until it's in. Wolf's phone not handy? Any phone can pick for them.
  • Best ball of each side wins. Every winner books the points:
  • Wolf & partner win 1 each — or the other two beat them for 2 each.
  • Lone Wolf wins 2 — or the field wins 1 each.
  • Blind Lone Wolf wins 3 — or the field wins 2 each.
  • Closest to the pin on a par 3: 1, whatever happens on the hole.
Hole 1 · par 4Nate is the Wolf. He passes on Greg's drive and takes Mike.
Nate 5, Mike 4 → 4. Greg 5, Tyler 5 → 5.
Nate & Mike win 1 point each.

Going alone, pushes and par 3s

Hole 2 · par 4Greg goes Lone Wolf and makes 4. Mike 4, Tyler 5, Nate 6.
A push — nobody scores.
Hole 3 · par 3Mike takes Tyler: 4 and 4 → 4. Nate 3, Greg 4 → 3.
Nate & Greg win 2 points each, and Nate is closest to the pin: +1.
Hole 4 · par 4Tyler calls Blind Lone Wolf and makes 4. Nate 5, Greg 5, Mike 6.
Tyler wins 3 points.

Closest to the pin needs the ball on the green; No one on green means no point, and it doesn't carry.

Ties carry over (option): a push carries, and the next hole that's decided pays × 2 — two pushes in a row, × 3. The closest-to-pin point never carries. With it on, hole 3 above pays Nate & Greg 4 points each.

The comeback on 17 and 18

With Last place is wolf on 17–18 on, the Wolf on the last two holes is the player with the fewest points, counted on the round's Scored as setting (net unless you change it). Tied for last? Whoever is earlier in the order. On a nine it's the last two holes of the nine.

After 16 holesNate 4, Greg 3, Mike 3, Tyler 2. Tyler is last, so Tyler is the Wolf on 17. He goes Lone Wolf and wins 2 — now 4.
Greg and Mike are now tied for last, and Greg is ahead in the order: Greg is the Wolf on 18.

Turn it off and the rotation keeps going: the first and second golfers get a fifth turn.

Handicaps

Play it net and the strokes come off the low man: the best player plays off zero, and everyone else gets the difference between their course handicap and the low man's — one stroke a hole on the hardest holes first, by the card's stroke index.

Course handicaps 5, 11, 13 and 8Nate plays off 0, Greg gets 6, Mike 8, Tyler 3.
On the number 1 handicap hole, Greg, Mike and Tyler each get a stroke.

The pick buttons show who gets a stroke on the hole, so the Wolf sees it before choosing. Course handicaps come from each player's index and tees, at the group's handicap allowance (100% unless you change it in setup).

💰The money

Wolf is points, and every player settles with every other player on the difference.

Nate +$6, Tyler +$2, Greg −$2, Mike −$6.

Set the $ per point under Settle up on the Standings screen; Battle Tees shows every pair there and never touches the money.

  • Ties carry over — Off.
  • Last place is wolf on 17–18 — On.

Set the points and the two rules before the first hole; they stay for the round. Every phone in the group plays with the same settings.

⚡How to win it

  • Know your Wolf holes. The rotation is fixed from the first tee. Being the Wolf on a reachable par 5 beats being the Wolf on the number 1 handicap hole.
  • The first pass is the big call. Pass on a good drive and you're gambling on the next two.
  • Go alone on position, not mood — when you're in the fairway and they're in trouble, or when you're behind late.
  • Read the dots. A high handicapper with a stroke on a hard par 4 is often the best partner on the tee.

Variations you'll hear about

Three or five players. Some groups play Wolf with a threesome or a fivesome. Battle Tees plays it with four, exactly.

🔥Why it's the most fun game in golf

Wolf front-loads the drama. Every single hole opens with a public decision that everyone watches you make — and then has to live with. There is no coasting, no waiting for the match to develop, no hole where nothing is at stake.

It also creates the two best sounds in a money game: the groan when the Wolf passes on somebody's drive, and the noise when a Lone Wolf pulls it off. Passing on your buddy's fairway finder and then watching the fourth player block one into the trees is the single funniest recurring event in golf.

Because the role rotates, everybody gets the hot seat an equal number of times. Nobody can complain about luck for long — you'll be Wolf on your own bad hole soon enough.
